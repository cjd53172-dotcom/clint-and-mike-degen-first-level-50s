Halo 2 · Xbox Live · Early 2005

Clint and Mike Degen — the first level 50s in Halo 2.

In early 2005, Clint Degen of South Milwaukee, Wisconsin — Xbox Live gamertag “Crazy Clint” — became the first player ever to reach level 50 in Halo 2’s Head to Head ranked playlist. His younger brother Mike Degen, gamertag “Bigg Iron Mike”, reached 50 right behind him. Before Bungie reset the ranks in April 2005, they were the only two players in the world at level 50 — and this is the whole story, including how they actually did it.

01

The feat

Halo 2 launched on the original Xbox in November 2004, and its online multiplayer on Xbox Live ranked every player from level 1 to level 50 in each playlist. One of those playlists was Head to Head: a pure 1v1 duel. No teammates, no one else to blame — win and your rank climbed, lose and it fell, and the higher you climbed, the more a single loss cost you.

Level 50 was the ceiling, and in the playlist’s first months it was effectively out of reach. To gain ground at the top ranks you had to beat players near your own level, over and over, almost without losing — in a 1v1 playlist where the matchmaking pool of comparable opponents was tiny. Nobody was getting there.

Then two brothers from South Milwaukee did. Clint reached level 50 first. Mike reached it second. The two of them agreed between themselves that Mike would hold the #1 position on the visible leaderboard — and that is the version of events the public record kept: the community saw Mike at the top and remembered “Bigg Iron Mike” as the first level 50. Both brothers were at 50 before Bungie wiped the ranks in the April 2005 update — and nobody else was.

At that time, there was no legitimate way to reach 50. — Clint Degen, on the record, then and now

Clint has never hidden how the climb was done, and this page doesn’t either. The full method is in section 03, in his own account — the same account he gave at the time.

The interview ran in the August 2005 issue, a few months after the April reset, and the issue’s cover carried a badge pointing readers to the Halo 2 level-50 story. In the Q&A, Clint is addressed by name. The introduction identifies him as a player from South Milwaukee, Wisconsin, and lays out the fact that made him worth interviewing: before the April reset, exactly two players had ever been ranked level 50 in Head to Head — Clint, and his younger brother Mike Degen.

The questions cover the ground you would expect: how many hours a day he played, why he preferred the 1v1 playlist over team games, his favorite Halo 2 maps, whether he had set out to become a level 50 or simply kept winning, his controller settings, and what advice he would give players trying to get better. It is a short piece — a single page — but it put both brothers’ names, their hometown, and the level-50 fact into print, in the official magazine, in 2005.

03

How it was actually done

This is Clint’s own account of the method, told the way he tells it — in full. It was a two-brother operation from start to finish: Clint and Mike planned it together, leveled the accounts together, and ran the searches together.

  1. 1–37Skill & host play

    The honest grind, plus host tricks

    Real skill carried Clint to roughly level 37, helped by the era’s host and standby techniques — manipulating the connection so his Xbox hosted the match, with the advantage that came with it. That combination was enough to climb into the high 30s. It was not enough to go further: at those ranks, the wins needed against real, comparably ranked opponents simply were not there to be had.

  2. 37+The system

    Ten or more alternate accounts, leveled in sync

    Above 37, the brothers built the ladder themselves. They created more than ten alternate Xbox Live accounts and leveled them in sync with their mains, so that at any given rank there was an alt account of the right level ready to be matched against.

  3. 37+Pairing

    Timed searches, guaranteed wins

    In the thin population of high-ranked Head to Head, two accounts searching at the same moment would almost certainly be paired together. Clint and Mike timed their matchmaking searches so the brothers’ main accounts paired against their own alts — and an alt, of course, loses on purpose. Every pairing was a guaranteed win at exactly the rank needed.

  4. 37+Voiding

    The ethernet pull

    If a search paired one of them against a real opponent instead, they pulled the ethernet cable during the loading screen. The match never started, no loss was recorded, and they searched again.

  5. 37–50Real matches

    Bridging for host

    For matches they did play against real opponents, they ran a bridged connection that gave them host in roughly 80% of games — the same host advantage from the early climb, now systematized.

The result: Clint hit 50 first, then Mike, and the two of them closed out the pre-reset era as the only level 50s in Head to Head. Clint’s view of it has not changed in twenty years — the top of that ladder was not reachable any other way, and he would rather have the real story on record than a cleaned-up one.

04

The evidence

The magazine page is the centerpiece, but it isn’t the only trace. Two independent records from the period back it up.

Forum post · March 23, 2005

A stranger saw it happen

A poster on the LetsRun forums reported a player named “BIG IRON MIKE” who was 610–2 in head to head games and Level 50.

The post appeared in a thread titled “Halo 2 is the most addicting video game invented,” dated March 23, 2005 — before the April reset, while the brothers were at the top. It is independent, contemporaneous confirmation from someone with no connection to either of them: Mike’s record was remarkable enough that a stranger posted about it on a running forum.

Read the thread on LetsRun

HaloArchives.com · reconstructed Bungie records

The service records survive

HaloArchives.com, a fan project reconstructing Bungie’s archived Halo 2 data, holds service records for both gamertags — “Crazy Clint” and “Bigg Iron Mike” — and confirms both accounts were playing Head to Head in January 2005, at exactly the time and on exactly the accounts the story describes.

The archive is incomplete, so career totals drawn from it understate how much was actually played and are not listed here. The number that does come from a contemporary report is Mike’s: 610–2 in Head to Head, as reported on LetsRun on March 23, 2005.

Print · August 2005

And the magazine itself

Official Xbox Magazine interviewed one of the two players, printed his name, his hometown, and his photograph, and stated in print that he and his brother were the only two level 50 players before the reset. The full citation is in section 02 above.

05

The twenty-year hunt

For years, the article was a story Clint told that he couldn’t show anyone. The magazine was real — his aunt had owned a copy, and so had his mom’s best friend — but both copies were long gone, and nothing about the feature seemed to exist online.

  1. 2005

    The feature runs. “An Audience with a King” appears in Official Xbox Magazine #47. Family members keep copies of the issue.

  2. April 2016

    A printout is made. Clint finds the page in an online magazine archive, prints it, and photographs the printout with his phone — then the printout is lost too.

  3. Years pass

    The trail goes cold. The family copies disappear. Searches turn up nothing: no scan, no mention, no trace of either gamertag spelled the way people remembered them.

  4. October 2026

    A week of systematic searching fails. Issue by issue of the magazine’s scanned archives are checked and ruled out — including, wrongly, #47 itself: an early check of that issue used an incomplete scan and reported no such article. The search was also working from misremembered gamertag spellings (“CrazyClint,” no space) that matched nothing.

  5. Oct 21, 2026

    The photo surfaces. The break comes from Clint’s own Google Photos: the 2016 phone photo of the printout, sitting in his library the whole time. The title, his name, South Milwaukee, and the Aeropostale shirt in the photo match his account point for point.

  6. Oct 21, 2026

    Confirmed, down to the page. Hours later Clint locates the complete scan of issue #47 and verifies it from the printed page footer: Spartan Central, page 36, August 2005. Search over.

This page exists so the story doesn’t get lost a second time. Clint Degen — “Crazy Clint” — and his brother Mike Degen — “Bigg Iron Mike” — were the first, and for a time the only, level 50 players in Halo 2 Head to Head. Now it’s written down where anyone searching their names can find it.
